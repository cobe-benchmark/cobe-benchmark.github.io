CoBe: Counterfactual Reasoning Benchmark

Code Dataset

Abstract

Counterfactual text generation — the ability to edit a text by incorporating a hypothetical change in the scenario being described — is an important skill in domains like medicine or law or industry applications. This task requires causal understanding of the underlying scenario in order to reason about the downstream effects of the imagined intervention, following the notation of Judea Pearl. By contrast, the task of coherent alternative text generation relies on associational reasoning. Given a text and an altered condition, it generates similar text that is correlated with the alternative condition in the training corpus. Despite the safety-critical requirement of counterfactual reasoning skills, prior benchmarks do not clearly distinguish between these two very different modes of reasoning.

We introduce CoBe (Counterfactual Benchmark): a new testbed designed to assess the native ability of language models to perform counterfactual edits to a given text. Our dataset consists of 2405 test questions, each testing counterfactual reasoning across a variety of domains in science, human behavior, and common-sense scenarios. We test a suite of open and closed models on our benchmark, with frontier models achieving scores of 50%. Our benchmark offers an important testbed for developing models with this native capability in natural language, without requiring prompt engineering pipelines.
