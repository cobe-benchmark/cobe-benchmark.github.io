Key findings

What CoBe reveals about today's models

Counterfactual editing looks easy for humans, yet it stress-tests a capability that scale alone has not solved.

Far from solved

Even the strongest model tops out at 55.7%, with frontier models averaging just 53.9% — barely past chance on a task humans find intuitive.

Scaling isn't enough

Gemma-2-27B lands within ~4% of flagship models, while 7–8B open models hover near chance. Bigger weights don't reliably buy causal reasoning.

Distinct error fingerprints

Models fail differently: some optimize for correlational consistency, others drop background facts or break on numerical changes. CoBe exposes these idiosyncrasies.

Causal structure matters

Accuracy drops on correlated, fork and diamond graphs — models mistake correlation for causation and fail to propagate effects across branches.

The task

Counterfactual edit vs. associational rewrite

Both answer a "what if", but only one reasons about cause and effect. Prior benchmarks blur the line — CoBe pulls them apart.

Counterfactual · Pearl Level 3

Reason about a hypothetical intervention

Fix the factual scenario and its hidden background factors, imagine an intervention, then rewrite only what is causally downstream.

"Kennedy died on Nov 22. What would have happened had Oswald not shot him?"

Answer: He lives — Oswald acted alone, so with no shooter, Kennedy survives.

Associational · Pearl Level 1

Rewrite by statistical correlation

Condition on an alternative event and generate text that is merely likely to co-occur with it in the training corpus — no causal model required.

"Given that Kennedy died, what happened if Oswald did not shoot?"

Answer: Someone else shot him — a plausible correlate, but not a counterfactual edit.

Abstract

CoBe in one paragraph

Counterfactual text editing — the ability to edit a text by incorporating a hypothetical change in the scenario being described — is a core reasoning skill, particularly relevant in domains like medicine, law and business. This task requires causal understanding of the underlying scenario. By contrast, associational text generation relies on correlational reasoning: given a text and an altered condition, it generates similar text that co-occurs with the alternative condition in the training corpus. Despite the safety-critical requirement of counterfactual reasoning skills, prior benchmarks do not clearly distinguish between these two very different modes of reasoning.

We introduce CoBe (Counterfactual Benchmark): a new testbed designed to assess the native ability of language models to perform counterfactual edits to a given text. Our dataset consists of 2,377 questions, each testing counterfactual reasoning across a variety of domains in science, human behavior and common-sense. We test a suite of frontier and smaller models on our benchmark, with frontier models only achieving an average score of 53.94%.
